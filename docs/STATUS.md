## What's built

- `backend/`: FastAPI and SQLAlchemy Core. With `DATABASE_URL` in its environment it uses the `undercover` schema on Supabase (at most 4 connections, waiting up to five minutes for one); without it, SQLite (`backend/undercover.db`, or the file in `UNDERCOVER_DB`), which the tests use. Both are seeded from `data/` on first start. The answer key (`data/evals/`) goes into tables of its own and never into the queue.
  - Read: `list_claims` (the queue, never evaluation copies), `get_claim` (with the claim's `cost` and `seconds`), `get_policy`, `get_claim_history` (earlier claims only, no personal details), `get_instructions` (the claim agent's), `list_handlers`, `list_steps`
  - Documents: `list_sections`, `read_section` and `search_documents` (`backend/documents.py`)
  - Payout rules: `calculate_payout` (`backend/payout.py`). The approval limit comes from `data/company.json`.
  - Write: `add_step`, `record_decision`, `pay_claim`, `sign_off`, `run_agent`. A repeat of a decision or payment already recorded counts as done and returns the claim; a different one is refused with 409. `sign_off` and `run_agent` refuse an evaluation copy.
  - Cost and time: `record_model_call` (tokens and OpenRouter's cost of one model call) and `record_work_time` (one stretch of the agent's work on a claim, which leaves out the wait for a sign-off). Both keep a run id only for a dispatch or an evaluation.
  - Runs: a run is a `dispatch`, an `evaluation` or a single `claim`, and is `running`, `finished`, `failed` or `stopped`. `create_dispatch` and `create_evaluation` (called by the agent's service) refuse with 409 while another dispatch or evaluation is running. `start_dispatch` and `start_evaluation` ask the agent's service through `backend/agent_service.py` and return 503 "The agent isn't running." if it can't be reached. `list_claims_to_work` gives a run's claims (in progress first, then waiting, oldest submitted first; an evaluation's own copies). `start_claim` starts a waiting claim in a run or joins a claim in progress to it, keeping its thread. `release_claim`, `start_claim_run`, `stop_running_runs`, `finish_run` (the backend builds the summary from its records), `get_run`, `get_latest_dispatch`.
  - Evaluations: `eval_claims` and `eval_decisions` hold the two labelled sets and the handlers' decisions, read only by the copying and the scoring (`backend/scoring.py`). An evaluation works copies in the `claims` table (`evaluation_id`, `source_id`, id `<evaluation>-<claim>`). `list_evaluations` and `get_evaluation` score a run from the records: `decision_accuracy`, `accuracy`, the cost of mistakes, the straight-through rate, cost per claim, time per claim and run time.
  - Claim states: `waiting`, `in_progress`, `awaiting_sign_off`, `decided`, `needs_handler`. A claim stays `in_progress` while the agent owes it work. Approving over $1,000 moves it to `awaiting_sign_off`, and `pay_claim` refuses it until a handler signs off. `sign_off` records who and when, moves the claim to `in_progress` and tells the agent's service in a background task.
- `backend/instructions/claim.md`: the claim agent's instructions.
- `agent/`: the UnderCover Agent on OpenRouter (`openai/gpt-6-luna` with low reasoning effort, or `MODEL_NAME` with none).
  - `agent/dispatcher.py`: the dispatcher, a LangGraph graph with no model: `list_claims`, one `Send` per claim, and a `work_claim` worker with `max_concurrency` set to N, a `RetryPolicy` for `ModelRateLimitError` and `ModelTimeoutError`, and an `error_handler` that leaves a claim in progress. It saves no progress of its own. A claim that fails with another error goes back to the queue and the worker tries it once more.
  - `agent/claim.py`: the claim agent, a LangGraph graph: `agent` and `tools` in a loop, then `decide`, `wait_for_sign_off` (`interrupt()`) and `pay`. Its model nodes record each call's tokens and cost. `work_claim(claim_id, run_id)` is the one function the dispatcher calls: it carries on a claim in progress or starts a waiting one. A rate limit or timeout that outlasts the graph's retries is raised without settling the claim. A copy held for sign-off counts as finished and its checkpoints are deleted.
  - `agent/checkpoints.py`: a `PostgresSaver` for each claim run on one shared pool of at most 4 connections (made once, under a lock), or one in-memory saver without `DATABASE_URL`.
  - `agent/service.py`: the agent's service (port 8002): `run_dispatch`, `run_evaluation`, `run_claim`, `resume_claim` and `health`. On startup it marks any dispatch or evaluation still running as stopped.
  - `agent/main.py`: the model, the backend client, and Langfuse tracing. Each claim's work, before and after a pause, is in one Langfuse session named after its thread, and its traces are tagged with its dispatch or evaluation id.
  - `uv run -m agent CLM-1001` works one claim against a running backend.
- `dashboard/`: React, Vite and TypeScript in the UnderCover brand. It starts in the system's light or dark setting, and the Dark mode button switches it.
  - `#/`: the queue, a count for each state, Claim agents at once (1 to 25, starting at 3) with Start a dispatch, and the latest dispatch's panel: each claim with its state, start, time and cost, the dispatch's model cost and time, and its summary when it finishes
  - `#/claims/<id>`: the claim, its policy, the decision, the payment, Run agent, what the agent read, its steps, and Sign off for payouts over $1,000. A copy shows a note and a link back to its evaluation, with no Run agent or sign-off.
  - `#/scoreboard`: start an evaluation (set, whole or tiny, N) and the list of evaluations, newest first, with every measure
  - `#/evaluations/<id>`: the evaluation's measures and claims, with the handler's decision beside the agent's, misses marked and each claim linked to its page
  - `#/instructions`: the claim agent's instructions, read only
- `e2e/`: Playwright tests. They run their own backend (port 8001, `e2e/test.db`, rebuilt each run), dashboard (port 5174) and a stand-in for the agent's service (`e2e/agent-stand-in.js`, port 8003, which records what the backend sends it, bodies included), so they never call a model.

## Running it

- Put `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, `DATABASE_URL` (the Supabase session pooler string) and the Langfuse keys in `.env`.
- `npm run seed` rebuilds the database on Supabase (and drops the agent's checkpoints), scoreboard included. Stop `npm run dev` first.
- `npm run dev` starts the backend (port 8000), the agent's service (port 8002) and the dashboard (http://localhost:5173), and passes `.env` to the first two. On Windows `uvicorn --reload` can stall after a change; stop the whole stack and start it again to pick up code changes.
- Choose Claim agents at once and press Start a dispatch on the queue page, or start an evaluation on the Scoreboard. `MODEL_NAME=openrouter/free` in `.env` is free, but allows about 20 requests a minute, so use a small N.
- `npm run check` runs every check. Move `.env` out of the way first: the tests never need Supabase, Langfuse or OpenRouter.

## Live runs (GPT-6 Luna, low reasoning, Supabase)

The first live run found two bugs, which are fixed: the service made a dozen connection pools instead of one when 16 claims asked at once, using up the free plan's 15 session connections and leaving two claims waiting five minutes for one; and a claim's traces got no `langfuse_tags`, because a claim worked inside the dispatcher has a parent run, so the tags are now set with `propagate_attributes` as the session is. The database was reseeded a second time to run the sequence again.

- **N=3, stopped.** A dispatch at N=3 was stopped after 4 claims were decided by killing the reloader and its worker on port 8002, which made `npm run dev` stop the others. It left 2 claims in progress (CLM-1004 and CLM-1012, attempt 0), 3 waiting for sign-off, 4 decided and 16 waiting, and its run still marked running. After the restart the service marked it stopped.
- **N=25.** The next dispatch worked 18 claims: the 2 in progress and the 16 waiting. They all started within 13 milliseconds of each other. CLM-1004 and CLM-1012 carried on with their threads (`...-RUN-b13966eb-1`), attempts unchanged, one decision and one payment each. The dispatch took 85.5 seconds, against 1,083.9 seconds of claim time, and cost $0.0321 in model calls. No claim failed for want of a connection.
- **Slower claims at N=25.** A claim took 60 seconds on average, against 29.5 seconds at N=3. Langfuse shows where it went, for the same 6 model calls: the model took 15.5 seconds (11.7 at N=3), the backend's lookups 16.5 seconds (3.7), and the rest of the graph, which is posting steps and usage and writing checkpoints, about 28 seconds (4). With 4 connections in the backend and 4 in the checkpointer, 18 claim agents queue for them: a backend call takes 0.15 to 0.25 seconds on its own, over the session pooler. Raising the connections is not possible on the free plan, so N=25 trades about twice the time per claim for a run of about a minute and a half.
- **Connections.** `postgres` connections in `pg_stat_activity`, through the Supabase MCP server, were 10 at the start of the N=25 dispatch and 9 at the start of the first evaluation, which is the two processes' 4 each plus `pg_net` and the query's own. The pooler keeps connections of a stopped process for several minutes (29 were counted in the first run with the pool bug). I sampled only at the start of each run.
- **Sign-off.** A sign-off during the N=25 dispatch paid in about 6 seconds (the brief asks for five); with nothing running it paid in 2.5 seconds.
- **Evaluations.** The evaluation set at N=25 (EVAL-f47fa467): decision accuracy 76%, accuracy 72%, cost of mistakes $1,110.00, straight through 88%, $0.0019 a claim, 1 minute 36 seconds a claim and 1 minute 56 seconds for the run. The held-out set's 5 `tiny` claims at N=5 (EVAL-6d3fa555): 100%, 100%, $0.00, 80%, $0.0019, 46.5 seconds and 57.0 seconds. A third `tiny` run (EVAL-4b8a7edc) checked the tags. The queue was 25 claims, 23 decided and 2 waiting for sign-off, before and after, with no copies in it.
- **Langfuse.** Through the observations API, the 5 claims of EVAL-4b8a7edc are found together by its tag. The N=25 dispatch was run before the tag fix, so its traces carry their sessions but not its tag.

## Notes

- Unused: `policy_wording_short.md`, `knowledge.json` and `security/`.
- Two processes can't see each other's locks: `uv run -m agent` carrying on a claim while the agent's service is working the same claim would work it twice. The backend still stops a repeat decision or payment, so nothing is decided or paid twice.
- A claim worked by a process that is killed loses the time of its last stretch of work, which is recorded when the stretch ends.
- LangGraph 1.2.12 re-raised a node's error after its `error_handler` ran when several claims ran in a step; the project uses 1.2.24, where the handler's result stands.
- Stopping `npm run dev` from a tool on Windows can leave uvicorn and vite running; stop the whole process tree (`taskkill /PID <pid> /T /F`).
- Supabase's advisor flags the `undercover` tables for having row-level security off. Supabase's public API only serves `public` by default, so they aren't reachable that way.
